\begin{abstract}\noindent
POSIX lexing by Brzozowski/Antimirov derivatives stays fast only if the
simplified derivative states stay small.  This note gives a fully
self-contained statement of the one inequality this project still has to
prove: after one step of the strongly simplified Antimirov-style row
transition, the total size of the \emph{deduplicated} set of opened linear
forms is cubic in the size of the original regular expression.  All
definitions below are faithful translations of the checked Isabelle/HOL
definitions in the repository (exact names in the table of
Section~\ref{sec:names}); every fact labelled ``checked'' is proved in
Isabelle with no \texttt{sorry}.  Section~\ref{sec:known} records what is
already proved, Section~\ref{sec:refuted} what is \emph{refuted} and must not
be attempted again.
\end{abstract}

\section{Regular expressions and size}\label{sec:rexp}

Regular expressions over an alphabet $\Sigma$ (Isabelle skeleton type
\thy{rrexp}, restricted to its classical---``legacy''---fragment%
\footnote{The formal skeleton carries three additional proof-only
backreference constructors (\thy{RBACKREF4}, \thy{RHALF}, \thy{RRESIDUE}).
They have no partial derivatives and are excluded here by the predicate
\thy{legacy\_rrexp}; the open problem lives entirely in the classical
fragment.}):
\[
r \;::=\; \ZERO \;\mid\; \ONE \;\mid\; c
 \;\mid\; r_1\cdot r_2 \;\mid\; \Sigma[r_1,\dots,r_n]
 \;\mid\; r^{*} \;\mid\; r^{\{n\}} \qquad (c\in\Sigma,\ n\in\mathbb{N})
\]
where $\Sigma[\,\cdot\,]$ is a \emph{list} alternative (ordered, possibly
with duplicates) and $r^{\{n\}}$ is bounded repetition (\thy{RNTIMES}).
Size $\rs{\cdot}$ and list size $\rss{\cdot}$ (\thy{rsize}, \thy{rsizes}):
\[
\rs{\ZERO}=\rs{\ONE}=\rs{c}=1,\quad
\rs{r_1\cdot r_2}=1+\rs{r_1}+\rs{r_2},\quad
\rs{\Sigma rs}=1+\textstyle\sum_{r\in rs}\rs{r},
\]
\[
\rs{r^{*}}=1+\rs{r},\qquad
\rs{r^{\{n\}}}=1+\rs{r}+n,\qquad
\rss{rs}=\textstyle\sum_{r\in rs}\rs{r}\ \ (rs\ \text{a list}).
\]
Note the repetition counter $n$ itself counts toward size.  Nullability
$\nullable$ (\thy{rnullable}) is standard; $\nullable(r^{\{0\}})$ holds.

\section{The transition pipeline}\label{sec:pipeline}

\paragraph{List helpers.}
$\flts$ (\thy{rflts}) deletes $\ZERO$ members and splices one level of nested
alternatives:
$\flts(\ZERO\#rs)=\flts\,rs$;\;
$\flts((\Sigma ts)\#rs)=ts\mathbin{+\!\!+}\flts\,rs$;\;
otherwise the head is kept.
$\nub$ (\thy{rdistinct}, with empty accumulator) keeps the first occurrence
of each member, preserving order.
The smart alternative constructor $\altsmart$ (\thy{rsimp\_ALTs}) maps
$[\,]\mapsto\ZERO$, $[r]\mapsto r$, and longer lists $rs\mapsto\Sigma rs$.

\paragraph{Sequence normalizer.}
$\seqnorm$ (\thy{rsimp4\_SEQ\_atom}) builds a right-associated sequence while
erasing units:
\[
\seqnorm(\ZERO,t)=\ZERO,\quad
\seqnorm(\ONE,t)=t,\quad
\seqnorm(p\cdot q,\,t)=\seqnorm(p,\seqnorm(q,t)),
\]
and for an atom $a$ (none of $\ZERO,\ONE,{\cdot}$):
$\seqnorm(a,\ZERO)=\ZERO$, $\seqnorm(a,\ONE)=a$, otherwise
$\seqnorm(a,t)=a\cdot t$.
On top of it, $\seqstar$ (\thy{rsimp7\_SEQ\_atom}) absorbs adjacent equal
stars:
\[
\seqstar(p^{*},p^{*})=p^{*},\qquad
\seqstar(p^{*},\,p^{*}\!\cdot k)=p^{*}\!\cdot k,\qquad
\seqstar(p,t)=\seqnorm(p,t)\ \text{otherwise}.
\]

\paragraph{Partial derivatives.}
The Antimirov partial-derivative list $\pder_c$ (\thy{rpder\_list}):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\pder_c(\ZERO)&=\pder_c(\ONE)=[\,],\qquad
\pder_c(d)=\text{if } c{=}d \text{ then } [\ONE] \text{ else } [\,],\\
\pder_c(\Sigma rs)&=\mathsf{concat}(\mathsf{map}\ \pder_c\ rs),\\
\pder_c(p\cdot q)&=\mathsf{map}\,(\lambda x.\,\seqnorm(x,q))\,(\pder_c\,p)
  \mathbin{+\!\!+}(\text{if }\nullable(p)\text{ then }\pder_c\,q\text{ else }[\,]),\\
\pder_c(p^{*})&=\mathsf{map}\,(\lambda x.\,\seqnorm(x,p^{*}))\,(\pder_c\,p),\\
\pder_c(p^{\{n\}})&=\text{if } n{=}0 \text{ then } [\,] \text{ else }
  \mathsf{map}\,(\lambda x.\,\seqnorm(x,p^{\{n-1\}}))\,(\pder_c\,p),
\end{aligned}
\]
and its normalized form
$\npder_c(r)=\mathsf{map}\,(\lambda x.\,\seqnorm(x,\ONE))\,(\pder_c\,r)$
(\thy{rpder\_norm\_list}).

\paragraph{Multi-step factored states.}
One factored step on a row list (\thy{afactored\_step} $=$
\thy{rpder\_norm\_rows}) and its iteration (\thy{afactored1}):
\[
\mathsf{step}_c(rs)=\nub\bigl(\flts\bigl(\mathsf{concat}
   (\mathsf{map}\ \npder_c\ rs)\bigr)\bigr),
\qquad
\Fact(r,\varepsilon)=[r],\quad
\Fact(r,\,s\,c)=\mathsf{step}_c(\Fact(r,s)).
\]
Note: \emph{no} strong pruning happens inside $\Fact$; the strong simplifier
enters only in the single step that the open problem measures.

\paragraph{Strong simplifier.}
The pairwise shared-suffix pruner (\thy{rsimpStrong\_prune\_pair\_raw}): an
earlier row $e$ prunes a later row $l$ by
\[
P(e,l)=
\begin{cases}
\seqstar\bigl(\altsmart(\,ms\setminus ls\,),\,k\bigr)
  & \text{if } e=(\Sigma ls)\cdot k \text{ and } l=(\Sigma ms)\cdot k,\\
l & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\]
where $ms\setminus ls$ removes from the list $ms$ every member occurring in
$ls$ (\thy{rprune\_eq\_against}), keeping order.
The scan (\thy{rsimpStrong\_prune\_rows\_raw}) folds $P$ over the already
emitted rows:
\[
\mathsf{scan}(seen,[\,])=[\,],\qquad
\mathsf{scan}(seen,\,r\#rs)=r'\#\,\mathsf{scan}(r'\#seen,\;rs),
\]
where $r'=\bigl(\mathrm{fold}_{e\in seen}P(e,\cdot)\bigr)(r)$, and
$\mathsf{prune}(rs)=\mathsf{scan}([\,],rs)$.
The strong simplifier $\Sstrong$ (\thy{rsimpStrong\_raw}):
\[
\begin{aligned}
\Sstrong(\ZERO)&=\ZERO,\quad \Sstrong(\ONE)=\ONE,\quad \Sstrong(c)=c,\qquad
\Sstrong(p\cdot q)=\seqstar(\Sstrong(p),\Sstrong(q)),\\
\Sstrong(\Sigma rs)&=\altsmart\bigl(\nub\bigl(\flts\bigl(\mathsf{prune}
  \bigl(\flts(\mathsf{map}\ \Sstrong\ rs)\bigr)\bigr)\bigr)\bigr),\qquad
\Sstrong(p^{\{n\}})=\Sstrong(p)^{\{n\}},\\
\Sstrong(p^{*})&=
\begin{cases}
\ONE & \text{if }\Sstrong(p)\in\{\ZERO,\ONE\},\\
q^{*} & \text{if }\Sstrong(p)=q^{*},\\
\Sstrong(p)^{*} & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\]

\paragraph{The strong one-step row transition.}
(\thy{rpder\_strong\_rows\_raw}; this is the object the problem measures.)
\[
\Dstep_c(rs)\;=\;\nub\Bigl(\flts\Bigl(\mathsf{prune}\Bigl(\flts\bigl(
  \mathsf{concat}\,(\mathsf{map}\ (\mathsf{map}\ \Sstrong\circ\npder_c)\ rs)
  \bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr).
\]
We also name the \emph{generated} rows before simplification:
$\gen_c(rs)=\mathsf{concat}(\mathsf{map}\ \npder_c\ rs)$.

\section{The ledger objects}\label{sec:ledger}

The \emph{opener} $\open$ (\thy{row\_dlforms}) unfolds a row into its
deduplicated linear forms\footnote{Recursion terminates by the size measure:
when $p \in ps$, $\rs{\seqstar(p,k)} < \rs{(\Sigma ps)\cdot k}$
(\thy{rsize\_rsimp7\_SEQ\_atom\_member\_lt\_RSEQ\_RALTS}).}:
\[
\open(\ZERO)=\emptyset,\qquad
\open(\Sigma rs)=\bigcup_{q\in rs}\open(q),\qquad
\open\bigl((\Sigma ps)\cdot k\bigr)=\bigcup_{p\in ps}\open\bigl(\seqstar(p,k)\bigr),
\]
and $\open(q)=\{q\}$ for every other shape.  For a row list,
$\open(rs)=\bigcup_{q\in rs}\open(q)$ (\thy{row\_dlformss}).
The \emph{set ledger} of a finite set $U$ of regexes
(\thy{rsize\_set}):
\[
\rss{U}\;=\;\sum_{q\in U}\rs{q}.
\]

\section{The open problem}\label{sec:problem}

\begin{conjecture}[set-ledger cubic gate]\label{conj:gate}
For every classical (legacy) regular expression $r$, every string
$s\in\Sigma^{*}$ and every character $c\in\Sigma$:
\[
\boxed{\;\rssb{\;\open\bigl(\Dstep_c(\Fact(r,s))\bigr)\;}\;\le\;
   2\,\bigl(\rs{r}+3\bigr)^{3}.\;}
\]
\end{conjecture}

In words: run the normalized Antimirov row automaton from $r$ over $s$, apply
\emph{one} strongly simplified step for $c$, open every resulting row into
linear forms, deduplicate \emph{across the whole union}, and sum the sizes of
the distinct opened rows.  The claim is that this total is cubic in the size
of the original expression --- uniformly in $s$, whose length does not appear
on the right-hand side.

\begin{remark}\label{rem:constant}
The constant $2(\cdot+3)^{3}$ is the project's working target; a checked
bound $K(\rs{r}+3)^{3}$ for any absolute constant $K$, or for the
$r^{\{n\}}$-free sub-fragment, is already valuable (see
Section~\ref{sec:accept}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
The inequality is stated for arbitrary $r$ in the consuming contracts; the
checked supporting facts that feed it (head linearity, the discharged gate
instance below) carry the hypothesis \thy{legacy\_rrexp}~$r$, so
Conjecture~\ref{conj:gate} should be read with that hypothesis.  Empirically
the left-hand side grows only \emph{quadratically} on exhaustive and random
probes (checkpoint 2026-06-12, ``multi-step union ledger probe'').
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[why this matters]
The conjecture is exactly the missing \emph{size half} of the per-step state
budget for the strongly simplified POSIX lexer.  The repository already
contains checked contracts
(\thy{rpder\_strong\_dcanon\_rows\_raw\_afactored1\_actual\_dlforms\_tight\_cubic\_contractI},
\thy{\dots\_budgetsI}) that take Conjecture~\ref{conj:gate} as their only
numeric assumption and return the full canonical-row contract: language
correctness of the canonical rows together with cubic bounds on their length,
cardinality, linear-term count, and total size.  The value/POSIX-correctness
side of the lexer is independently checked and waiting
(\thy{strong\_deferred\_memo\_lexer\_eq\_lexer} etc.).
\end{remark}

\section{What is refuted (do not attempt)}\label{sec:refuted}

\begin{theorem}[duplicated list cost is not polynomial; checked]\label{thm:listcost}
Define the \emph{list} opener $\Dlist$ by the same recursion as $\open$ with
concatenation instead of union (no deduplication), and the pipeline list cost
\[
\listcost(r,s,c)=\!\!\sum_{p\,\in\,\gen_c(\Fact(r,s))}\!\!
  \rssb{\Dlist\bigl(\Sstrong(p)\bigr)} .
\]
Let $\pi=(\ONE+a)\cdot(\ONE+b)$ and the tower
$T_0=k\cdot l$, $T_{n+1}=(\ONE+\pi)\cdot T_n$ (here $\ONE+x$ abbreviates
$\Sigma[\ONE,x]$; $a,b,k,l$ characters).  Then (all checked):
\[
\rs{T_n}=10n+3,\qquad
\mathsf{len}\,\Dlist(T_n)=3\cdot 2^{\,n}-2,\qquad
\Sstrong(T_n)=T_n,
\]
and behind one guard character $g$,
$\listcost(g\cdot T_{24},\,\varepsilon,\,g)\ge 3\cdot 2^{24}-2
 = 50{,}331{,}646 > 2(245+3)^{3}=30{,}505{,}984$,
refuting the cubic budget for $\listcost$
\hfill(\thy{afactored1\_strong\_dlform\_list\_cost\_cubic\_false}).
\end{theorem}

The mechanism: on $(\Sigma[\ONE,\pi])\cdot T$ the list opener recurses into
\emph{both} branches; the $\ONE$ branch collapses to $T$ and reproduces $T$'s
entire opened list, the $\pi$ branch walks it once more --- so the length
doubles per level, while the regex grows linearly.  The tower is a fixpoint
of $\Sstrong$, so simplification cannot rescue the cost.  Crucially, both
branches reach the \emph{same} tail rows: the deduplicated union merges what
the list multiplies, which is why Conjecture~\ref{conj:gate} survives this
family (quadratically).  Consequences and further checked refutations:

\begin{enumerate}
\item Any proof route needing a polynomial bound on $\listcost$,
$\Dlist$-length, or any per-row duplicated multiset is dead
(Theorem~\ref{thm:listcost}).
\item Generic square-sum wrappers
($\sum_q \rs{q}^{2}\le\rss{rs}^{2}$, or $\mathrm{len}\times\max^{3}$
products) are true but lose a degree: they yield quartic, not cubic, bounds.
\item The deep-frontier linear-card premise
$\#(\text{deep frontier})\le \mathrm{awidth}+\rs{r}+3$ is \emph{false} in
general --- $r^{\{n\}}$ multiplies alternation branches without paying into
the budget (checked witness with cardinality $49$ against budget $39$;
\thy{apder\_deep\_frontier\_linear\_card\_false}); only the
$r^{\{n\}}$-free version survives.  The front-linear variant is likewise
refuted (checked, 2026-06-12).
\item Generic owner-closure counting is exponential (checked abstract
counterexample, 2026-06-12).
\item $\Sstrong$ is \emph{not} pointwise $\open$-cost monotone: there is a
checked $p$ with
$\rss{\open(\Sstrong(p))} = 12 > 10 = \rss{\open(p)}$
(\thy{rsimpStrong\_raw\_row\_dlforms\_cost\_not\_monotone}).  Do not assume
simplification shrinks the ledger row by row.
\item A \emph{quadratic} static ledger is false: on the star--seq tower
$x_{0}=a$, $x_{i+1}=(x_{i}\cdot b_{i})^{*}$ the checked cubic bound
